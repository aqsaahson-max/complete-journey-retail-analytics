# Findings and business recommendations

| Finding | What managers can do | Interpretation limit |
|---|---|---|
| Grocery recorded $2,316,393.89, approximately 50.4% of total sales | Prioritize availability and category-level investigation in the largest sales department | Revenue share alone does not establish profitability |
| Recorded retail discounts total $791,650.64 | Examine discount concentration alongside category sales and, if available later, margin | This is not evidence that discounts caused extra sales |
| Average basket value is $29.49 across 155,848 baskets | Track basket value and basket counts together to distinguish more shopping activity from higher spending per basket | No good/bad judgment is possible without a relevant comparison |
| Households without recorded demographics account for about $1.97m, or 42.89%, of sales in the displayed grouping | Improve demographic coverage and retain Unknown in reporting | Known households may not represent all customers |
| January 2018 is partial | Compare complete periods or equivalent elapsed days before changing inventory or promotion plans | The apparent drop is not evidence of a full-month business collapse |
| High source quantities are concentrated in Fuel and Miscellaneous | Use sales and basket counts for cross-department comparisons; investigate unit definitions before quantity comparisons | Do not assume quantities across departments share a unit |

These recommendations are supported planning questions. The project has not measured commercial gains, campaign uplift or profit improvement from implementing them.
